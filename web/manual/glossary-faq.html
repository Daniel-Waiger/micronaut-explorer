Glossary

StudyThe whole plan you're building: a research question, a system or material, a comparison structure, and one or more measurements. See Study map.
MeasurementOne observation or analysis used to answer the study's question — its own readout, panel, specimen, and modality. In some fields this is called an assay. A study can hold several. See Measurements.
ReadoutWhat you're actually measuring. Choosing a recognized readout unlocks readout-specific control recommendations; an answer Micronaut doesn't recognize is respected as-is, never silently overridden. See Acquisition.
Comparison groupOne of your study's experimental groups (e.g. control vs. treatment), set once per measurement on its Samples & design. An observational study may have none at all. See Samples & design.
Experimental unitWhat counts as one independent replicate in your study (an animal, a dish, a field of view) — set once on Study map and used everywhere else that reasons about replication. See Study map.
ConditionOne concrete combination of group, sample, and replicate — the unit a single planned filename actually represents. A design with groups, factors, and replicates expands into a table of condition rows. See Samples & design.
ControlA comparison Micronaut proposes based on your panel and readout, each with a stated reason — rules propose, you dispose. A genuinely empty controls list is always labelled as such, never shown as silence. See Samples & design and Review.
Spillover (spectral overlap)A qualitative flag that two fluorophores' excitation or emission peaks sit close enough to risk bleeding into each other's channel — a peak-proximity check, not a full spectral-overlap integral. See Acquisition.
ProvenanceA tag on every field recording where its value came from — your own input, an example default, or a derived/suggested value — so a weaker source (like a free-text suggestion) can never silently overwrite something you typed yourself. See Research brief.
Conformance (export checks)The one composed pass/fail-style verdict — ready, needs review, or blocked — built from every check Micronaut already runs (naming format, design issues, spectral flags, path length, unanswered fields). It checks Micronaut's own rules, not scientific validity. See Export checks.
StageOne rung of the fixed five-step execution ladder every study sees on Review — idea, pilot, validate controls, fix acquisition settings, advanced modality — with any study-specific notes attached to the relevant rung. See Review.
ModalityThe type of microscope or imaging method used — confocal, widefield, STED, light-sheet, SEM/TEM, Raman, and so on. Drives which modality-specific advice Micronaut shows. See Acquisition.
PanelThe structured list of fluorophore channels in a measurement — each with a target and how it's conjugated (direct antibody, indirect, genetically encoded, a direct-binding probe, or a self-labeling tag) — the fact-precise alternative to typing markers as free text. See Acquisition.
RestoreThe rolling history of your last five distinct autosaved versions, reachable from the Utilities menu. Not a substitute for a real backup. See Restore.
Project backupA single downloadable .json file holding your entire study — the only copy that survives clearing browser storage or moving to a new computer. See Project backups.
Bench cardA compact, print-oriented single-measurement summary (channels, controls with reasons, two worked filename examples) — meant to sit next to you at the bench. See Review.

Frequently asked questions

Is my study data private?

Yes. Micronaut Planner has no server, no account, and no sync — your study lives only in this browser's local storage on this device, and nothing you type is ever uploaded anywhere. See Where your data lives.

Does Micronaut use AI?

Micronaut never calls a model itself — every result in the app is deterministic. Two features use a model's help, both one-way: Research brief's description review is a deterministic exact-text scan (marker aliases, replicate counts, magnification, unambiguous dates), and Review's "Copy prompt for your own LLM" hands you a block to paste into whatever LLM you already use — nothing it replies is read back into your study automatically. See Research brief and Review.

How do I move my study to another computer?

Download a project backup on the first computer (Settings or Utilities → Download/Export project backup), copy the file across by whatever means you'd move any file, then import it (Import project backup) on the second computer. See Project backups.

Why can't Micronaut email or sync my study for me?

There's no server behind the app that could send or sync anything on your behalf — everything runs locally in your browser. Feedback and backups are handed off through copy/paste and file downloads instead, which you control explicitly every time. See What "Copy feedback package" actually shares.

What does "alpha" mean here?

Micronaut Planner is an early preview shared for feedback. It's a planning aid, not a validated instrument model — always confirm real acquisition settings at the microscope, and treat the built-in modality guidance as provisional while it's still being reviewed by a microscopy specialist.

My filename preview shows "UNKNOWN" in several places. Is that an error?

No — it's a generated placeholder for a required field you haven't answered yet, not something Micronaut is rejecting. Fill in the field and the placeholder disappears from the preview. See Reading generated placeholders.

An unrecognized marker shows a warning on Data plan. Does this block my study?

No. Unless a lab-specific approved-marker list exists (there is none by default), any marker is accepted; even with a list, an unrecognized marker is a warning, not a blocking error, by default. See The naming rules.

Why does Review sometimes stop me from exporting?

Only a "Blocked" verdict (a real error somewhere in the study) refuses an export outright. A "Needs review" verdict just asks you to confirm before proceeding. See What happens when a study still needs review.
